# PPT Art Director

Choose one production route, state it in 2-4 lines, then execute immediately. For a deck of 12 slides or fewer, do not call `plan_task` unless substantial external research is itself a requested deliverable; route and build it directly. Do not stop at a plan and do not probe workspace/runtime Skill directories.

## Core production principle: per-slide customization

Per-slide customization is the default design method for every substantive audience-facing deck, regardless of page count. First decide what each page must make the audience understand, believe, compare, decide, or do; then give that page its own takeaway, evidence form, composition family, focal anchor, and task-local renderer. Reusable cover/chart/table/timeline/matrix/card functions are internal primitives, not a page-template library and not the final whole-deck layout engine. Visual consistency comes from shared tokens, typography, spacing, and art direction; page geometry comes from the content of that page.

Every content slide follows a claim–proof–implication hierarchy. Use a conclusion-led title that states what the audience should learn, not a generic noun such as “背景”, “方法”, or “数据”. Give one proof object—an actual chart, direct-label comparison, causal flow, architecture map, matrix, timeline, annotated exhibit, or evidence table—roughly 55–70% of the useful body canvas. Supporting prose is subordinate; add one short implication caption or conclusion rail only when it advances the story. A collection of equally weighted cards is not a proof object.

Freeze a role-based design system before page code. `art_direction` must name `palette_strategy`, `typography_strategy`, and `background_rhythm` in addition to the visual, composition, and asset strategies. Use one neutral canvas/ink pair, one primary accent, and only evidence-driven semantic colors. With no supplied reference, use at least 50pt for the deck title, 35pt for slide titles, 24pt for subheads, and 16pt for body copy; smaller text is reserved for compact source/footer annotations. Use contrast pages deliberately—normally a distinctive cover and closing plus at most a small number of section anchors—while keeping evidence pages quieter. This creates pacing without turning every page into the same dark panel or light card grid.

## Three-level contract

- **Level 3 / premium custom = `ppt-master`**. This is the first route for explicitly beautiful, premium, design-led, executive, board, investor, client, launch, sales, brand, or showcase work when strict native-template fidelity is not required. Use the complete upstream Strategist/Executor pipeline; do not scaffold a conservative Level-2 deck first.
- **Level 2 / regular custom = one task-specific editable per-slide build**. This is the normal default for substantive audience-facing work, including research, industry, technology, strategy, investor, client, sales, forum, and executive decks. Start from the scaffolded per-deck builder even when the deck is short. Its task-local `deck_spec.json` is the truth and narrative contract; bounded page functions in `build_deck.py` own the final composition of every slide. The standalone compact JSON renderer is reserved for an explicitly simple/fast/internal/recurring/fixed-schema job whose communication task genuinely fits a stable layout.
- **Level 1 / fallback structured (disaster-only) = `base-ppt`**. It is never the unspoken default. Use it directly only for explicit simple/fast/internal/recurring/fixed-schema work or compatible native placeholders. For substantive polished work, use it only after the selected quality route has one real build plus one targeted correction fail and every other applicable quality route is objectively unavailable or has also failed after one targeted correction.

## Level-2 start

For an explicitly simple/fast/internal/recurring/fixed-schema deck whose story fits the stable vocabulary—normally a concise 6–7 slide utilitarian deck—write a JSON object with `needs_customization=false`, `quality_profile="audience-facing"`, `brand`, and `slides`, then make one build call:

```text
python3 "<Runtime Directory>/scripts/build_task_pptx.py" --spec "<deck_spec.json>" --output "<output.pptx>"  # Linux/macOS
python  "<Runtime Directory>/scripts/build_task_pptx.py" --spec "<deck_spec.json>" --output "<output.pptx>"  # Windows
```

Supported slide kinds are `cover`, `statement`, `cards`, `split`, `process`, `metrics`, `chart`, `table`, `matrix`, `timeline`, and `closing`. A `cards` slide may use `conclusion: {title, body}` (preferred when exact conclusion wording is required) or `takeaway` for a visible high-contrast conclusion rail. A `process` slide may use `lateral` as a list of up to four cross-cutting capabilities. Timeline milestones accept the compact `{label, title, detail}` form and the richer `{label, milestone, points}` form; when `label` is `time · phase`, the renderer separates the time label from the phase title and keeps every point visible. A `closing` slide may add up to three `commitments` beside its three decision/action `items`, with optional `decisions_label` and `commitments_label`; all of that content is rendered at presentation-readable size. The command validates the specification, materializes a task-local editable renderer, builds the deck, and verifies file size and slide count. It is safe to correct the JSON once and rerun the same command when validation reports a specific schema error.

Do not use this **standalone** compact route for substantive audience-facing work merely because every page can be approximated by a supported kind or because the deck is short. Those decks first use the scaffolded per-deck route below. In either Level-2 route, use several different body compositions, avoid three consecutive pages with one skeleton, keep `cards` rare, and include explanatory evidence such as a chart, process, timeline, matrix, table, metric, diagram, or annotated exhibit when the story calls for it. Two consecutive evidence tables are acceptable when an explicit user-required table must be split to preserve all rows; content fidelity outranks cosmetic variety. A repeated-layout or tiny-text warning is a correction request, not “normal template reuse.”

## Decision order

1. If the user explicitly and positively requests `pptmaster`, `PPTmaster-main`, `generator: pptmaster`, `generator=pptmaster`, or the full upstream Strategist/Executor workflow, call `use_skill ppt-master` and run it end to end. A negated mention such as “不要用 PPTmaster” is not an override.
2. For `strict-existing`, inspect the user-supplied PPTX before selecting an engine. Use `base-ppt --template-mode strict` only when inherited placeholders can express the requested pages. Otherwise use an object-mapped task-specific native edit or report the fidelity limitation. Never send strict native-template preservation through PPTMaster and never overlay generic headers/cards while claiming fidelity.
3. Treat PPTMaster as the higher-end commercial route. Prefer it for premium executive, board, client, investor, sales, brand, launch, or showcase decks when visual storytelling is central, the content is sufficiently frozen, strict native-template fidelity is unnecessary, grouped/native SVG editing is acceptable, and the user did not ask for a fast draft. When selected, call `use_skill ppt-master` and run the complete upstream workflow; do not imitate it with a local builder.
4. If visual fidelity dominates and element-level editing is unnecessary, choose a matching HTML/canvas/deck Skill such as `frontend-slides`, `deck-open-slide-canvas`, `deck-guizang-editorial`, or `deck-swiss-international`, and disclose that packed slides may be image-backed.
5. When the higher-end commercial criteria in step 3 do not apply, default substantive audience-facing decks to the task-specific `python-pptx`/PptxGenJS scaffold. Use `deck_spec.json` to preserve facts and narrative intent, then write short page-specific functions for every slide. Reuse its stable chart, table, timeline, matrix, shape, and typography primitives for mechanics. This route remains natively editable. Architecture diagrams, flowcharts, relationship maps, annotated comparisons, custom infographics, and unusual focal compositions are normal page-design work, not reasons to flatten the page into a generic kind.
6. Call `use_skill base-ppt` when the user explicitly prioritizes simple or fast output, the artifact is internal or recurring, the source already has a stable report schema, or compatible native-template placeholders fully express the pages. Those cases permit direct Level-1 selection. For a substantive polished deck, do not call it merely because one quality route failed: repair that route once, then switch to the other applicable quality route (`ppt-master` or the task-specific native renderer). Only after both are objectively unavailable or still fail after their targeted correction may `base-ppt` be used as disaster recovery. State the concrete failures and downgrade reason in the final response.
7. Use `paper-operators-visual` only for a small number of concept/hero assets that materially improve the story.

Fallback in quality order: PPTMaster → task-specific native renderer → `base-ppt`. This list is a quality ranking, not permission to skip recovery. Start with the best-fit quality route; repair it once; try the other applicable quality route; use `base-ppt` only as the final disclosed disaster route. Do not skip directly to `base-ppt` merely because a higher route needs a task script, a command guess failed, a dependency probe failed, or the first build needs correction. Never downgrade silently.

Use the deck's job to break close route ties: executive/board/strategy decks need consulting-style evidence hierarchy; product launches need sparse high-impact storytelling (`ppt-keynote` when appropriate); editorial/culture narratives fit `deck-guizang-editorial`; rational factual narratives fit `deck-swiss-international`; training/research/government/data-dashboard decks prioritize legibility, evidence, and template fidelity over decoration. Editability is an output property, not a visual-quality tier. Record the chosen renderer and template intent separately in the internal handoff.

A source report containing many charts or tables is not automatically a utilitarian deck. If the user expects a presentation of the insights, a cumulative story, or an audience-ready transformation rather than a recurring report export, use the task-specific route.

Do not choose `base-ppt` merely because it is editable. Both the task-specific renderer and `base-ppt` can produce natively editable PowerPoint elements; choose between them by the communication job and required composition range.

`better-ppt` is a legacy metadata alias that resolves to `base-ppt`, not a second public route. New work must call `base-ppt` instead.

## Task-specific renderer rules

- Prefer standalone `build_task_pptx.py` only when the compact vocabulary fits **and** the user clearly requested simple/fast/internal output or the artifact is genuinely recurring/fixed-schema. A concise 6–7 slide count alone is not permission to use it. Keep the spec factual and audience-facing; do not emit empty pages or repeated placeholder markers. Every substantive audience-facing deck starts with the scaffold and receives per-slide customization.

- Do not recreate the design primitives from scratch. The Runtime Directory includes a tested task-specific `python-pptx` scaffold. Materialize it once, then edit only the task content region and the page-specific compositions:

```text

python  "<Runtime Directory>/scripts/scaffold_task_pptx.py" --builder "<workspace>/.openkitty/storage/build_deck.py" --output "<output.pptx>"  # Windows
```

The scaffold copies `pptx_design.py` beside the task builder and supplies palette, typography, margins, header/footer, a claim-led decision frame, semantic evidence legend, proportional bars, chips, cards, integer-EMU geometry, bounds checks, and edges-first connectors. Do not read or rewrite these bundled files before executing the scaffold command. After scaffolding, read the generated builder once, make one complete edit of `deck_spec.json`, and use anchored `edit_file`/`multiedit` changes to implement the page functions. Do not inspect the design module. The task-local builder receives page-specific edits for every slide, including ordinary evidence pages; unsupported diagrams, asymmetric focal anchors, visual reveals, and annotated exhibits simply require more expressive page functions. Stable renderers are reusable mechanics, not permission to ship repeated card grids.
- The copied design module also supplies `native_chart`, `editable_table`, `timeline`, and `matrix`. Use these for mechanics instead of rewriting OOXML or table/chart styling in every task. Timeline preserves the explicit milestone order; matrix requires explicit `label`, `x`, and `y` values with normalized coordinates in `0..1`. Neither helper may infer content, chronology, categories, or coordinates.
- `native_chart` accepts `column`, `bar`, `line`, `pie`, `doughnut`, `stacked-bar`, and `stacked-column` (plus the equivalent hyphen/underscore aliases). Prefer these stable editable chart types instead of probing the runtime after a build failure.
- The stable header API is `header_footer(slide, brand=..., title=..., section=..., page=..., total=..., theme=THEME, dark_mode=False)`; pass the page title so the shared navy rail owns the headline. `dark_mode` remains accepted for compatibility. Do not inspect its signature or create a probe script.
- For vertical text alignment, pass `anchor=MSO_ANCHOR.TOP`, `anchor=MSO_ANCHOR.MIDDLE`, or `anchor=MSO_ANCHOR.BOTTOM`. Never emit `anchor="top"`, `anchor="middle"`, or `anchor="bottom"` in task code. The scaffold normalizes legacy string aliases before the `python-pptx` boundary, but generated code must use the enum.
- For option comparison, recommendation, investment committee, site selection, vendor selection, prioritization, or another decision deck, read exactly `<Runtime Directory>\references\decision-deck-patterns.md` after scaffolding. It is a composition reference, not a fixed ten-slide template. Use `decision_frame(slide, deck_title=..., takeaway=..., brand=..., page=..., total=..., source_note=..., theme=THEME)` when a repeated claim-and-evidence rail helps the audience; keep each page body task-specific. Use `proportional_bar(slide, x, y, w, h, value, max_value, color=..., track=...)` for comparable bounded bars. Do not copy a prior business case or force its page order onto unrelated decks.
- Give every slide its own narrative job and composition. Avoid repeating giant empty cards or turning every page into a dashboard.
- Make the visual plan executable: `layout_family=editorial|spotlight|comparison|metric` plus optional `primary_share` and `mirror` yields `planned_layout(page)` regions. Place native evidence and explanatory copy into those regions; they draw no containers. Use the visual-director contract for region meanings. Preserve task-specific geometry for other compositions and supplied templates. Never make every page use one family merely because a helper is available.
- Treat composition as a first-class decision, not a synonym for slide kind. Across the deck, deliberately vary focal scale, alignment, evidence form, and density while preserving one visual system. At least a third of substantive body slides should have a clear dominant visual or explanatory structure rather than equal-weight tiles or raw lookup tables.
- Separate manuscript thinking from page styling. First transform source material into one core insight, its supporting evidence, and the visual material needed for each page; only then choose geometry. Treat images, diagrams, charts, and annotated exhibits as evidence-bearing content, not decoration. Generic business stock art, meaningless AI illustration, and repeated icon grids do not satisfy an asset strategy.
- Use the composition family to express the communication job: an asymmetric hero for a decisive claim, a directional diagram for mechanism, a chart for magnitude or comparison, a timeline for change, a matrix for trade-offs, an annotated exhibit for proof, and an editorial image-text composition for narrative. Do not select a visual skin or page family from industry keywords alone.
- Before writing the spec or page code, freeze a compact slide-architecture table with `slide`, `narrative job`, `takeaway`, `evidence form`, `composition family`, and `focal anchor`. For an ordinary 8-slide audience-facing deck, use at least four body composition families; for 9–12 slides, use several genuinely different families. Two consecutive pages may share a family when content fidelity requires it, but never repeat one body skeleton across three pages. Never delete, merge away, or replace a user-required fact, metric, condition, table row, or acceptance criterion merely to satisfy composition variety. This is a content plan, not a fixed template or permission to invent facts.
- Build a cumulative narrative rather than an agenda-shaped inventory. Use takeaway titles; make evidence answer a question raised by the prior slide and create the need for the next one.
- Use tables when the audience must look up exact values. Prefer a timeline for change over time, a matrix for categories or trade-offs, a chart for comparison, and a focused callout for the implication. Avoid consecutive tables unless an exact required table needs two pages to remain readable; in that case preserve every row and give the pages clear continuation titles.
- Never invent a chart note merely to fill its side panel. When no source-grounded implication exists, omit `note`; the stable chart renderer will use the full content width instead of showing an empty “关键解读” block.
- If the brief says management still needs to confirm a priority, acceptance rule, pilot team, budget, owner, or target, keep that decision visibly `待确认`/`管理层确认后确定`. Do not silently choose a quarter, team count, target, or other decision value.
- Establish a coherent visual system before page code: topic-appropriate palette, Chinese/Latin font pairing, grid, title rail, footer, and a small set of reusable primitives. Record a one-sentence internal palette rationale tied to the subject, source visuals, audience, or supplied brand. When no source brand or topic palette overrides it, use the approved regular consulting baseline: deep ink-navy cover and rails, warm gold keylines, white body canvas, restrained beige panels, and stable red/green semantic accents. Preserve this grammar without turning every page into the same blue-and-gold template.
- The scaffold also exports `GOOGLE_CLEAN_THEME` as an approved bright baseline. Select it when the user explicitly requests Google/谷歌风、清爽白底、明亮产品风，or when a modern product explainer clearly calls for that direction. It uses white and soft gray canvas, Google blue hierarchy, generous whitespace and crisp keylines; green, yellow and red are semantic status/comparison colors only. Do not imitate Google logos or trademarks, scatter decorative colored dots, or turn every page into a Material UI card grid. Formal board, finance and consulting decks remain on the ink-navy/gold baseline unless the user asks otherwise.
- The Level-2 baseline is deliberately flat and editorial: square or nearly square evidence regions, dark top rail plus thin keyline, compact footer, strong Chinese typography, generous margins, and page-specific chart/timeline/matrix/comparison compositions. Avoid rounded tile dashboards, emoji, floating pills, decorative dots, and repeated giant cards unless the content specifically requires them.
- Keep normal body copy at a presentation-readable size (normally at least 16–18 pt on a 16:9 slide), and do not solve density by shrinking four long columns, a nine-node timeline, or a large table onto one page. Split or prioritize when the rendered page cannot be understood at ordinary slideshow scale.
- Generation is cross-platform and must remain pure `python-pptx`/PptxGenJS: never make the PPTX build depend on PowerPoint COM. The bundled design system selects Microsoft YaHei/Arial on Windows, PingFang SC/Helvetica Neue on macOS, and Noto Sans CJK SC/Liberation Sans on Linux so local preview metrics remain readable. Native PowerPoint or LibreOffice is a QA renderer only, not a generation dependency.
- Do not treat “technology” as permission for a navy + neon cyan + violet + gold + green + red stack. Do not use high shape count, repeated cards, glowing circles, pills, or decorative dots as a proxy for visual richness. Premium work must obtain its character from the visual thesis, scale, imagery or explanatory diagrams, typography, whitespace, and page-specific composition.
